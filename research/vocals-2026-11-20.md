# Human-sounding vocals: what we learned (19-20 November)

The goal, in the user's words: "for simple throwaway slop we can use a model; for bangers we need control, not a
slot machine". ACE-Step "does too much": it makes a song, not a clean vocal. The Maya and Qwen voices are "where
the vocal wins are". This note records the route we built, every A/B the user judged, and what is still weak.

## The route: our notes, a real singer, a Maya or Qwen voice

1. **We write the melody and the words** in the song script: one token per grid slot, "-" holds, "." rests, one
   word per note ("+" holds a word over the next note). The pitch we write is the pitch she sings.
2. **Lem the Shapeshifter DS 2.5** (DiffSinger, through warble; voice mode Civet) sings it (`ds.Part`). The
   voicebank's terms allow conversion and commercial use. A release must name it exactly "Lem the Shapeshifter
   DS 2.5". Hoshino Hanami's terms forbid conversion, so `parts.Band` refuses a timbre, stack or harmony for her.
3. **SoulX-Singer SVC** (audio.cpp) converts the take into a Maya or Qwen voice (`ds.convert`, `timbre`). The
   reference is the TTS voice saying `ds.REF_TEXT`. The notes, words and timing stay ours. Only the sung stretches
   are converted (`ds.sung`), so 8 bars in four voices take about 3 minutes.
4. **The producer's rack**:
   - doubles from takes 2 and 3 in two more voices, a few ms late and panned wide (`stack`);
   - a soft octave and a channel vocoder (`vfx.py`);
   - a harmony a third away in a fourth voice where a line has choir (`harmony`). It replaces the Piper choir.
   - words made louder (`accent`).
5. **The singer made alive** (see below): warble's rule tuner, more vibrato, tension, breaths, dynamics.

Any template song gets the whole route from `parts.Band(S, PROG, diva_voice="ds:lem", ...)`. The Badge has the
same chain in its own code.

## Why the singer sounded dead, and the fixes

- **No pitch model.** warble reports Lem as `"pitch": "rule (no dspitch)"`. Every note was a flat line with a
  plain bend between notes: warble analyze measured 0 cents of vibrato on every note. The conversion keeps the
  source pitch, so the Qwen voice was flat too.
  - Fix: `ds.tuned()` runs `warble tune auto <score> rule` and copies its controls (`pts`, `vbr`, `drift`) onto
    our JSON notes. warble tunes only its text format, which reads some words (such as "time") as directives, so
    the controls are copied back note by note.
  - The rule tuner gives a scoop or a bend with overshoot into each note by its interval, vibrato on long notes
    with a late onset, a slow drift, and a small fall at the end of a phrase. Measured: vibrato 19-34 cents.
- **Vibrato depth and tension**: `vib=1.6` scales the vibrato; `tension=25` (the DiffSinger tension offset, Lem
  has a tension embed) gives more power.
- **No breaths**: a DiffSinger "AP" note (a breath, up to 0.3 s) now goes in the rest before each phrase.
- **No dynamics**: Lem has no energy model, so every note was equally loud. `ds.Part.dynamics()` sets each
  phrase's high notes to 1.0 and its low notes to 0.8. A note longer than 0.5 s swells from 0.85 to 1.0 by 60 % of
  its length, then eases to 0.9.
- Template songs with Lem default to all of this (`parts.LEM`): `DS_PITCH=rule DS_VIB=1.6 DS_TENSION=25
  DS_BREATH=1 DS_DYN=1`. Each switch makes a test render; set it to the old value for an A/B.

## Writing a melody for this singer

- Range: Lem is realistic from A2 to D5. Keep the steady notes around G4-A4. Use D5 at most, and E5 only as a rare
  peak. "Too high" was the main fault of the rehearsal song.
- A short word on a high or fast note slurs. Give consonant-heavy and stressed words a quarter note.
- Rests and short notes make a hook punch. An accent of 3-4 dB on the hook word helps.
- Check every line with Whisper on a `SOLO=vox` preview (`asr.hear`). A misheard word is usually a short word on a
  high note.
- Shape the melody with Lem alone (`DS_TIMBRE= DS_STACK= DS_HARMONY=`, fast), then render the whole chain once.

## Genre rules from the ears

- **90's mellow house: sparse.** An empty groove (kick, clap, swung hats, a round bass, one Rhodes comp) with one
  simple vocal on top. No doubles, no harmony, no vocoder, no crowd calls, no layers of organ, strings and shaker.
  Long stretches without words; at most one spoken or whispered line. `stack=""`, `harmony=""` in `parts.Band`.
- **Eurodance: the full stack.** Lead `qwen_diva`; doubles `sing_qwen_pop` and `sing_maya_pop`; octave and vocoder;
  a harmony; an MC in a Maya voice; crowd shouts.
- **Phone speakers.** The user listens without headphones. Check mono: the energy at 300-4000 Hz should not drop
  by more than 1.5 dB in mono, and the L/R correlation should stay above 0.6. Every song so far passes: Badge D
  -0.6 dB / 0.89, Can You Tell? -0.3 / 0.93, Common Ground 0.0 / 0.99.

## Still weak ("far from ideal")

- The pitch curve is rules, not a learned performance. A DiffSinger bank with its own pitch model (dspitch) and
  terms that allow use as a tuner could give a neural pitch curve (warble's `dspitch` tuner).
- The timbre reference is a speaking voice. A sung reference sounded "through a pipe". Another sung reference, or a
  longer one, is not tested yet.
- Whisper still mishears some words. In Common Ground, "common ground" comes out as "come and ground", and in the
  verse "words" as "woods" and "wire" as "choir".
- The Badge keeps its own approved chain: flat pitch, no breaths. Moving it to the new singer would be a new A/B.
